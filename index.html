Decreasing Housing Costs for the American People

A plain-language, section-by-section review of the executive order, including practical implications for homeowners, homebuyers, and real estate professionals.

Overview

The order seeks to lower homeownership costs by reducing certain mortgage-related barriers, exploring automated valuation and title-risk tools, supporting factory-built housing, and directing lower interest rates on specified federal direct-loan programs. Many provisions depend on subsequent agency action and do not immediately establish a borrower entitlement.

Section 1 | Purpose

Policy objectives: reduce regulatory barriers and mortgage-origination costs; use advanced risk and property-value assessment technologies (described in the order as “Super Intelligence”); and encourage competition and innovation in housing and mortgage markets.

Section 2 | Proactive Cancellation of Excessive Mortgage Insurance

  • FHFA may direct Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (the “Enterprises”) to create a pilot program, if the Director makes the required best-interest determination. The pilot would apply only to mortgages originated after the pilot is established.
  • The Enterprises would periodically review eligible single-family loans they own, guarantee, or securitize, using automated valuation models and other data to identify borrowers who may qualify for mortgage insurance cancellation.
  • Cancellation would require sufficient equity based on current property value, financial safety and soundness, consistency with contractual obligations, satisfactory payment history, and compliance with all other applicable requirements.
  • Loan servicers would, where lawful and operationally feasible, promptly notify potentially eligible borrowers and reduce unnecessary paperwork, appraisal expenses, and administrative obstacles.

Practical significance: Home appreciation may help qualifying borrowers reach an equity threshold, but the order does not cancel mortgage insurance automatically today or override existing eligibility rules.

Section 3 | Standardizing the Permitting of Homes

The order creates a Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation, co-chaired by HUD and FHFA, with participation from Agriculture, Commerce, and Veterans Affairs.

Within 180 days of October 22, 2026, the Task Force must submit recommendations addressing:

  • Harmonizing specifications, certifications, inspections, and approvals across federal housing and housing-finance programs.
  • Reciprocal recognition of licenses, certifications, approvals, and inspections to prevent duplication.
  • Removing unnecessary federal requirements and repetitive reviews that slow automation, modular construction, and standardized production.
  • Using existing federal financing, insurance, guarantees, purchasing, and other authorities to support affordable manufactured and factory-built housing.
  • Promoting manufacturer competition while maintaining safety, quality, durability, and consumer protections.

The Task Force must also evaluate incentives tied to federal grants for state and local standardized permitting and licensing, and prepare legislative recommendations. Participating agencies must take appropriate implementation action within their authority.

Practical significance: The goal is to reduce delays and production costs for factory-built housing. It does not itself preempt all state or local permitting rules.

Section 4 | Reducing Excessive Title Insurance Costs

Subject to statutory, safety-and-soundness, and FHFA determinations, the Director may direct Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to expand the title acceptance pilot into an ongoing nationwide program. For eligible refinances, an FHFA-approved automated title review or other title-risk assessment could replace a lender’s title insurance policy.

The FHFA Director must consider:

  • Allowing low-title-risk refinance loans with loan-to-value ratios above the current 80% program limit.
  • Removing geographic restrictions and approving additional title-risk assessment providers.
  • Waiving appraisals, income re-verification, and other documents for qualifying limited cash-out refinances that lower monthly payments, where existing Enterprise data justify the waiver.
  • Reducing or eliminating loan-level price adjustments and other Enterprise fees on those refinances.

The Director must require program cost savings to be passed on to borrowers and disclosed by lenders.

Practical significance: This is aimed at eligible refinance transactions, not a general elimination of title insurance in purchases. Replacing a lender’s policy also does not necessarily eliminate the need for an owner’s title policy or resolve existing title defects.

Section 5 | USDA and VA Direct Loan Rates

The Secretaries of Agriculture and Veterans Affairs are directed, consistent with applicable law and available appropriations, to reduce interest rates on single-family home loans made directly by their departments.

Practical significance: The provision concerns departmental direct loans, not every USDA-guaranteed or VA-guaranteed mortgage issued by private lenders. The order does not specify a new numerical interest rate.

Section 6 | General Provisions

  • Existing legal authority of agencies and the Office of Management and Budget remains intact.
  • Implementation must comply with applicable law and available appropriations.
  • The order creates no independently enforceable private legal right or benefit.
  • FHFA bears the publication costs of the order.

Key Takeaways for Real Estate Practice

  • Buyers: Mortgage insurance relief could become easier for some future eligible borrowers, but do not promise automatic cancellation.
  • Builders and developers: Watch for Task Force recommendations that could improve manufactured and modular housing approvals and financing.
  • Refinancing homeowners: Eligible borrowers may eventually see lower title, appraisal, documentation, and pricing costs.
  • USDA/VA clients: Verify whether a loan is a qualifying direct loan and check current agency rates before quoting savings.
  • Implementation watch: Track FHFA pilot rules, Enterprise servicing guidance, Task Force recommendations, and USDA/VA rate announcements.
